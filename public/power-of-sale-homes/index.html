Why buyers look at them

Real homes, motivated sellers.

A power of sale is a home sold by the lender after the owner falls behind on the mortgage. It is listed on MLS® like any other home. What makes it different is the seller: a lender that wants to be repaid, not to hold out for top dollar.

The same goes for homes where the price has already come down, or that have been listed for two months or more. The seller has seen what the market will pay and is ready to talk.

01

Priced to sell

Lenders price to attract offers, and many power of sale homes sell for less than similar homes nearby.

02

A seller that looks at the numbers

No emotional pricing and no attachment to the house. The lender wants a clean offer and a timely close.

03

Often vacant

Many are empty by the time they are listed, which can make visits and closing dates easier to arrange.

04

Room to negotiate

A price that has already dropped, or weeks on the market, usually means a seller open to a sensible offer.

Worth knowing on power of sale homes: they sell as is, with no warranties from the seller, the lender adds its own terms to the offer, and it can take a few days to answer. I walk you through all of it, so you can offer with confidence.

Common questions

Straight answers.

Can I see the list of homes now?

The area snapshot above shows how many are listed and the price ranges. The homes themselves, with my notes on each, go to the buyers I work with, once we sign a buyer representation agreement.

Why include homes with price drops?

A price that has come down, or two months or more on the market, tells you the seller has seen what buyers will pay. Those sellers are often the most open to a fair offer, and most buyers scroll past them. I look at why each one has sat before you spend time on it.

Are power of sale homes cheaper?

Often. Lenders price to sell, and many power of sale homes sell for less than similar homes nearby. Every home is different, so we look at the numbers together before you offer.

Do I need a pre-approval first?

It helps. Lenders like clean offers, and a pre-approval lets you move quickly when the right home comes up.

Do I still need a lawyer?

Yes. As with any purchase, you'll have your own real estate lawyer for closing.

What is a buyer representation agreement?

A written agreement between you and the brokerage that sets out what I do for you, for how long, in which areas, and how I am paid. Working together starts with one. We go through it on the call, and you sign only once you are comfortable with it.

Is this private?

Yes. What you tell me about your budget and plans stays between us.
